# Polished model essay (≈280 words)

Many people now prefer giving cash on special occasions such as weddings or birthdays rather than choosing a physical gift. This trend is largely driven by convenience and flexibility, but if it replaces personal gestures altogether, it can weaken social bonds. Overall, I view it as a mixed development that requires thoughtful execution.

The first reason for the shift is practicality. Money is universally useful: recipients can put it towards pressing expenses—such as mortgage payments, tuition fees, or everyday bills—or simply save it for a larger purchase. In this sense, cash avoids waste and ensures the gift has real value. A second factor is uncertainty about personal tastes. Choosing a present for someone whose preferences, size or lifestyle you do not know can be stressful and time-consuming. Cash eliminates the risk of buying something unwanted or unusable. Finally, modern payment methods and social norms have normalised monetary gifts, making them quick to send and easy to receive, especially when people live far apart.

However, there are drawbacks. A carefully chosen present can carry emotional meaning that cash often lacks. A handmade item or a book inscribed with a thoughtful note can become a lasting memory, whereas money tends to feel transactional. If monetary gifts become the default, celebrations risk losing their personal touch and may be interpreted as perfunctory rather than heartfelt.

In conclusion, giving money is popular because it is convenient, efficient and flexible. Yet, to prevent it from eroding the warmth of special occasions, givers should consider pairing cash with a personalised message or a small symbolic item. Used in this way, the trend can remain practical without sacrificing sentiment.
