# **Model Essay**

In many countries today, the number of students choosing to study science subjects at university has declined. This trend can be attributed to several factors, including the difficulty of such courses, unattractive working environments, and comparatively lower salaries. It also brings serious consequences for both economies and societies.

One major reason students avoid science is the level of academic challenge. Disciplines such as physics, chemistry, and engineering require strong mathematical and analytical skills, and many students find the workload too demanding. For those who do not possess a natural aptitude, years of study can feel discouraging and unrewarding. Another factor is the working environment linked to some scientific fields. Jobs in manufacturing or chemical industries, for example, often involve exposure to noise, hazardous substances, or shift work, which makes them less appealing. Finally, financial considerations play a role. Careers in business, law, or finance often offer higher starting salaries and faster career progression, which naturally attracts ambitious young people.

The decline in science enrolment has significant social and economic implications. Modern economies depend heavily on technological innovation, which is driven by professionals trained in STEM fields. If fewer students pursue these disciplines, industries may face talent shortages, leading to slower innovation and weaker competitiveness. In addition, companies may relocate production to countries with a stronger scientific workforce, resulting in higher unemployment and growing inequality at home.

In conclusion, the difficulty of scientific studies, poor working conditions, and lower salaries discourage students from pursuing science at university. The shortage of skilled professionals will pose a serious threat to long-term economic growth and social stability.
