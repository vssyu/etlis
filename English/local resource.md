model essay:
Art has always been a fundamental part of human culture, but opinions differ on whether it is an ability that everyone can cultivate or a gift reserved for a select few. While some argue that artistic expression is within everyone’s reach, others believe that true art requires innate talent. This essay will discuss both perspectives before presenting my own view.

On the one hand, many people believe that everyone is capable of creating art. This view is grounded in the idea that art is a universal form of self-expression rather than a technical skill reserved for professionals. For example, children often draw, sing, or dance without formal training, yet their creations can still be meaningful and emotionally powerful. Moreover, with the growth of accessible art tools and online tutorials, anyone can learn basic techniques and develop their creativity. In this sense, art is seen as an inclusive activity that can enrich lives and foster personal growth.

On the other hand, some argue that only individuals with special talents can produce genuine art. They contend that while anyone can learn the basics, the ability to create truly original and impactful work requires natural aptitude, a refined sense of aesthetics, and exceptional creativity. For instance, not everyone can compose music like Mozart or paint like Van Gogh, regardless of years of practice. According to this view, talent acts as a foundation that cannot be entirely replaced by training or effort.

In my opinion, while talent can certainly enhance artistic quality, it is not a prerequisite for creating art. Art should not be narrowly defined by technical mastery or commercial value; it is a deeply personal form of communication that anyone can engage in. Just as not every runner must be an Olympic athlete to enjoy the benefits of running, not every artist needs to be a genius to find meaning in their work or to move others. Therefore, I believe that art is accessible to all, even if only a few reach the highest levels of excellence.

In conclusion, while natural talent may give certain individuals an advantage in the arts, the ability to create is inherent in everyone. Encouraging people to express themselves artistically can enrich both individual lives and society as a whole.
