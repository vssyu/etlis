## model essay

Some people argue that it is acceptable to use animals for human benefit, while others believe exploiting them is morally wrong. Both perspectives have valid arguments, but I personally think animals can be used for human purposes under strict regulation.

Supporters of using animals highlight the essential role they play in advancing human life. In medical research, for instance, animal testing has enabled the development of life-saving treatments and vaccines. Without testing new drugs on animals first, the risks to human health would be significantly higher. Moreover, animals also contribute to human survival through food production, which is a traditional practice in almost every culture. From this perspective, human benefit is considered a priority, making animal use both necessary and justifiable.

On the other hand, opponents claim that animals have intrinsic rights and should not be treated as tools. They argue that causing pain or restricting freedom to living beings for the sake of human advantage is unethical. Furthermore, unnecessary exploitation—such as cosmetic testing or entertainment industries—reflects human greed rather than survival needs. In addition, modern technology provides alternatives like computer simulations and lab-grown meat, which reduce the need to harm animals.

In my view, while some use of animals is inevitable, it must be carefully limited. Governments should regulate industries to ensure animals are treated humanely and experiments are only conducted when no alternatives exist. This balanced approach respects human needs while minimizing animal suffering.

Although opinions differ, I believe that animals can be used for human benefit only under strict ethical guidelines. This way, society can benefit from scientific progress without causing unnecessary harm.
