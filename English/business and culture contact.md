## model essay

Business and cultural links between countries have expanded rapidly in recent decades. Supporters argue that cross-border exchange fuels economic growth and enriches people’s lives, while critics fear it erodes national identities. I believe the benefits are substantial, but they must be balanced with active measures to preserve local languages, traditions, and arts.

On the positive side, deeper international business ties create jobs, raise productivity, and expand consumer choice. Firms gain access to larger markets and new technologies, which can lower costs and lift wages. Cultural exchange also broadens horizons: foreign films, music, and literature expose audiences to fresh perspectives, fostering empathy and creativity. Students who encounter other cultures often develop stronger communication skills and a more global outlook—attributes that are valuable in modern workplaces.

Nonetheless, the risks are real. When a few global languages and brands dominate screens and storefronts, smaller cultures can be sidelined. Young people may favour international media over local storytelling, and minority languages may be spoken less at home and in schools. Commercial pressures can also standardise tastes, reducing the visibility of regional crafts and festivals. Without deliberate support, this “cultural flattening” can weaken people’s sense of heritage and belonging.

In my view, the goal is not to slow exchange, but to shape it. Governments and communities can protect identity while embracing openness by funding local arts, setting quotas for domestic content, supporting bilingual education, and encouraging businesses to partner with local creators. Such policies ensure that global flows bring opportunity without washing away distinct traditions.

In conclusion, increasing business and cultural contact brings clear economic and social gains, yet it can threaten national identities if left entirely to market forces. With smart cultural policies and education, countries can enjoy the benefits of openness while keeping their unique voices strong.
