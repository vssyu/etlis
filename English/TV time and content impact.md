## model essay 

model essay:
Television can shape children’s behaviour in two main ways: through the conten they watch and through the amount of time they spend watching. Both matter, but I believe content exerts the stronger influence, while sensible limits on screen time are still necessary.

Those who prioritise content point out that children are highly impressionable. Through modelling and imitation, they absorb the values, language, and problem-solving styles they see on screen. Programmes that glorify aggression or mock others can normalise unkind behaviour, whereas prosocial shows encourage empathy and cooperation. Because these messages shape what children consider acceptable, guidance on what to watch—age ratings, parental co-viewing, and discussions about on-screen choices—has a direct effect on behaviour.

Others argue that time is the decisive factor. Excessive screen time displaces sleep, outdoor play, reading, and face-to-face interaction, all of which are essential for healthy development. Even educational programmes can be harmful in excess if they crowd out these activities. Moreover, long stretches of passive viewing are linked to poorer attention and self-regulation, so time budgets and regular screen-free periods protect daily routines and mental focus.

In my view, content should come first: a few minutes of inappropriate material can do more harm than a longer period of well-chosen programming. That said, families and policymakers should address both dimensions. Governments can enforce clear age classifications and watershed rules, and platforms can strengthen parental controls. Parents can create a simple family media plan that pairs careful content selection with reasonable time limits and co-viewing, so children learn to think critically about what they see.

In conclusion, while both what children watch and how long they watch affect behaviour, content carries the greater weight. With wise content choices and balanced time limits, television can support rather than undermine children’s development.
