## Model Essay


Technology is a double-edged sword that can both suppress and stimulate criminal activity. While digital tools have expanded law-enforcement capabilities, they have also created new opportunities for offenders. Overall, I believe technology reduces crime when it is deployed with strong governance and public oversight.

On the one hand, technology can deter, detect, and disrupt traditional crimes. Ubiquitous CCTV and smarter lighting raise the perceived risk of being caught, which discourages opportunistic offending. Modern forensics—from DNA analysis to phone and payment-trail mapping—helps investigators identify suspects more quickly and accurately. In addition, data analytics can guide patrols to high-risk locations and times, allowing limited resources to be used more efficiently. When these tools are paired with clear legal safeguards and human judgment, they tend to prevent crimes and improve clearance rates.

On the other hand, technology has enabled entirely new forms of offending. The internet lowers barriers to cybercrime such as phishing, identity theft, and ransomware, allowing offenders to target thousands of victims at minimal cost. Encrypted messaging, anonymizing networks, and deepfake tools complicate detection, while online marketplaces facilitate the trade of stolen data and illegal goods. Even in the physical world, offenders adapt—using drones to scout targets or jammers to disable alarms—showing that innovation does not belong exclusively to the police.

In my view, the net impact of technology depends on how societies govern it. Strong data protection, transparent algorithms, and independent oversight can prevent abuses and bias, while investment in digital literacy, cyber-hygiene, and international cooperation can shrink offenders’ room to operate. In short, technology is most effective when combined with sound institutions and informed citizens.

In conclusion, although technology empowers both sides, robust design and regulation allow it to tip the balance toward lower crime overall. The goal is not more technology but better technology, embedded in accountable practice.
