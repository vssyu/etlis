**model essay:**

People define success in very different ways. Some argue that a successful life is built on attitude—qualities like hard work, grit and determination—whereas others judge success by outward markers such as wealth and physical appearance. In my view, while money and looks can open doors, genuine success should be measured primarily by inner fulfilment, character and contribution.

Those who emphasise attitude point out that determination transforms potential into performance. A growth mindset, disciplined practice and resilience after failure help people develop expertise and earn trust. For instance, employees who consistently deliver, learn from feedback and support teammates are more likely to be promoted into leadership roles. Crucially, these traits are within an individual’s control and remain useful even when circumstances change.

By contrast, some people equate success with possessions and appearance. Wealth can buy freedom of choice—better housing, healthcare and education—and an attractive appearance may confer social advantages in competitive settings. However, these indicators are unstable. Fortunes can be lost, beauty fades, and both can be inherited rather than earned. Moreover, judging success this way encourages comparison and anxiety rather than meaningful progress.

I believe a more robust definition centres on “inner success”: living by one’s values, maintaining strong relationships, and making a positive impact. Attitude-based virtues—perseverance, integrity and self-discipline—are the engines of this kind of success. Paradoxically, they also tend to produce external rewards over time: diligent people often create value, which brings financial security, and healthy routines can improve appearance without obsessing over it.

In conclusion, while material resources and appearance can be helpful, they are unreliable yardsticks. The most convincing measure of a successful life is the purposeful pursuit of worthwhile goals, sustained by determination and hard work, and reflected in personal growth and service to others.
