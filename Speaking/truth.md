

## Part 2:

**Cue Card Topic:** {{Describe a time when you told your friend an important truthYou should say}}

**You should say:**
- Who your friend is
- What the truth was
- What your friend's reaction was 
- And explain why you think it was important to tell your friend the truth

**My Answer (Bullet Point Outline):**

One time I told an important truth to my close friend Alex, who has been one of my best friends since high school. He was preparing for a job interview and had sent me his résumé to check. While reading it, I noticed that he had exaggerated some of his skills, such as claiming he was fluent in a foreign language when in fact he could only handle basic conversations.


I decided to be honest with him, even though I was worried it might upset him. I told him directly that including false information could harm his chances if the interviewer asked questions he couldn’t answer. At first, he was a bit embarrassed and tried to explain that “everyone does it,” but then he realized I was right. After a short silence, he thanked me for pointing it out and said he would rather present himself honestly than risk being caught lying.

I think it was important to tell him the truth because honesty builds trust not only between friends but also in professional life. If I had stayed quiet, he might have embarrassed himself in the interview or even damaged his career later. By being truthful, I helped him present a more authentic version of himself and maintain his integrity.

## Part 3: Two-Way Discussion

**1. Do you think it’s more important to win a game or follow the rules in sports?**
I believe following the rules is more important. Winning is only meaningful if it’s achieved fairly. If players break the rules just to win, the spirit of competition is lost and the victory becomes meaningless. In fact, respecting the rules also shows respect for your opponents, which is a fundamental value in sports.

---

**2. Sometimes people should tell lies. Do you agree?**
Yes, I think in certain situations small lies are acceptable. For example, if telling the truth would hurt someone’s feelings unnecessarily, a “white lie” can protect their emotions. Another example is in emergencies, where lying might calm people down or prevent panic. However, in serious matters like work, law, or relationships, honesty should always come first.

---

**3. How do you know when others are telling lies?**
It’s not always easy, but there are some signs. People who lie often avoid eye contact, hesitate in their speech, or give inconsistent details. Sometimes their body language, such as fidgeting or crossing arms, reveals nervousness. That said, these clues are not always reliable, so I think intuition and context also play an important role in judging.

---

**4. Do you think we should tell the truth at all times?**
In principle, yes, because truth builds trust and credibility. However, in real life, it’s not always practical to be 100% honest. There are situations where telling the truth might cause unnecessary conflict or harm. So I would say we should aim to be truthful most of the time, but also balance honesty with kindness and empathy.
